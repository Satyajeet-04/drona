1. Title of the Project
   •   Smart Ticket Understanding Engine: An AI-Powered NLP System for Intent
       Classification, Sentiment Analysis, SLA-Based Prioritization and Automated Routing
       of Customer Support Tickets
Project Guide: Dr. Parvez, Associate Professor, Department of CSE


2. Abstract
Customer support centers receive large volumes of unstructured tickets through emails, chats
and web forms, and manual triage of these tickets is slow, inconsistent and difficult to scale.
This often leads to Service Level Agreement (SLA) violations, especially in FinTech
environments where payment failures and security issues need immediate attention. This
project proposes the Smart Ticket Understanding Engine, an AI-powered system that
automatically classifies each ticket into one of 10 enterprise business categories, analyzes
customer sentiment and urgency, assigns an SLA-based priority from P0 to P3, and routes the
ticket to the appropriate support queue. Three approaches are evaluated: TF-IDF with Logistic
Regression as a baseline, a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM), and a fine-tuned DistilBERT
transformer, which is further optimized with ONNX INT8 quantization for low-latency CPU
inference. A human-in-the-loop Generative AI component produces contextual draft replies
that support agents can review and edit before sending. The system is implemented in Python
with FastAPI for backend services, Streamlit for the triage dashboard, SQLite/PostgreSQL for
storage, and Docker, MLflow, Evidently AI and Google Cloud Run for deployment,
experiment tracking and monitoring. The proposed system is expected to improve routing
accuracy, reduce manual triage effort and accelerate responses to critical issues.


3. Introduction
   •   Background: Enterprise and FinTech support teams receive tickets through emails,
       chat, web forms and other channels. Each ticket is unstructured free text that a support
       agent must read, understand, categorize, prioritize and route to the right team. Natural
       Language Processing (NLP) can automate this understanding step.
   •   Need for the work: Manual triage is time-consuming and subjective, so similar tickets
       are labeled and prioritized differently by different agents. Delays in triage directly
       increase response time and can breach SLAs.
   •   Current challenges: Ticket text is noisy (typos, mixed tone, missing context), volumes
       fluctuate, and urgency is often implied rather than stated. In payments and banking, a
       failed transaction or a security alert must reach the right team within minutes, while a
       general enquiry can wait.
   •   Importance of the proposed system: The Smart Ticket Understanding Engine
       combines intent classification, sentiment analysis and SLA-based priority assignment
       in one pipeline, and adds human-in-the-loop draft replies. It aims to reduce triage effort,
       improve routing consistency and speed up handling of critical issues while keeping
       agents in control.

4. Problem Statement
   •   Support organizations need to convert a continuous stream of unstructured tickets into a
       category, a sentiment, an SLA priority (P0–P3) and a destination queue accurately and
       in near real time. Manual triage does this slowly and inconsistently, leading to
       misrouted tickets, delayed responses and SLA violations.
   •   Limitations of existing approaches: Rule and keyword based routing is brittle and
       fails on paraphrased or noisy text. Many published systems address only classification
       and ignore sentiment, urgency and SLA logic [1], [2], [3]. Large transformer models
       such as BERT give strong accuracy but are costly to serve on CPU-only infrastructure
       [4], [5].
   •   Research gaps: (i) few end-to-end pipelines that jointly cover intent, sentiment,
       priority and routing; (ii) limited comparison of classical, recurrent and transformer
       models on the same ticket data; (iii) limited attention to quantized transformer inference
       for low-latency CPU deployment [6]; (iv) safe use of generative drafting with human
       review in support workflows [8].

5. Objectives
   •   To develop an AI-powered engine that understands support tickets and classifies them
       into 10 enterprise business categories.
   •   To analyze customer sentiment and urgency for every ticket.
   •   To assign an SLA-based priority from P0 (critical) to P3 (low) and route each ticket to
       the appropriate support queue.
   •   To compare TF-IDF with Logistic Regression, BiLSTM and fine-tuned DistilBERT
       using accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1-score and latency.
   •   To optimize DistilBERT with ONNX INT8 quantization and reduce CPU inference
       latency and model size with minimal loss of accuracy.
   •   To provide human-in-the-loop Generative AI draft responses that agents review before
       sending.
   •   To deploy the system as containerized microservices with experiment tracking and
       data-drift monitoring.

7. Proposed Methodology
      •   The system follows an end-to-end NLP pipeline: text preprocessing, tokenization,
          feature extraction, intent classification, sentiment analysis, priority assignment and
          automated routing, followed by optional GenAI drafting. Each stage is described below.
      •   Data Collection
              o Public banking and customer-service intent data (Banking77 [3]) is used as the
                  base and mapped to 10 business categories (for example Payment Failure,
                  Refund/Chargeback, Account Access, Fraud & Security, Card Services,
                  Loan/EMI, KYC/Verification, Billing & Charges, Technical/App Issue,
                  General Enquiry).
          o The data is supplemented with public support-ticket datasets and carefully
              generated, reviewed synthetic tickets so that every category and priority level is
              represented.
   •   Data Preprocessing
          o Lower-casing, removal of HTML, signatures and boilerplate, masking of
              sensitive tokens (account numbers, phone numbers, e-mail), normalization of
              whitespace and repeated characters, and stratified train/validation/test splitting.
   •   Feature Engineering
          o TF-IDF word and character n-grams for the baseline; pretrained word
              embeddings and padded token sequences for BiLSTM; WordPiece subword
              tokens for DistilBERT.
          o Extra signals such as sentiment score, urgency keywords, channel and presence
              of monetary or security terms feed the priority engine.
   •   Model Design
          o Model 1: TF-IDF with Logistic Regression (baseline). Model 2: BiLSTM
              classifier. Model 3: fine-tuned DistilBERT [5], exported to ONNX and
              quantized to INT8 for CPU inference [12].
          o Sentiment and urgency are predicted alongside intent. A priority engine
              combines category, sentiment, urgency and SLA rules to output P0–P3, and a
              routing table maps category and priority to a support queue.
          o A GenAI component drafts a contextual reply from the ticket, category and
              approved response templates; agents must review, edit and approve every draft.
   •   Training and Testing
          o Models are trained on the training split, tuned on the validation split and
              evaluated once on a held-out test split. Experiments are logged with MLflow
              [9].
   •   Evaluation Metrics
          o Accuracy, precision, recall, weighted and macro F1-score, confusion matrix,
              priority-assignment accuracy, per-ticket latency (ms), throughput and model
              size (MB), plus data-drift reports from Evidently AI.

8. System Architecture / Framework
   •   The architecture diagram below shows the workflow from ticket ingestion to agent
       review.
              Fig. 1. System architecture of the Smart Ticket Understanding Engine

   •   Ingestion Service (FastAPI): receives tickets from email, chat and web forms through
       REST endpoints and stores them.
   •   Preprocessing Module: cleans, masks and tokenizes text for the models.
   •   Intent Classifier: predicts one of 10 categories using the selected model (ONNX INT8
       DistilBERT in production).
   •   Sentiment & Urgency Analyzer: detects customer tone and urgency cues.
   •   SLA Priority Engine and Router: assigns P0–P3 and places the ticket in the correct
       support queue.
   •   GenAI Draft Generator: creates a suggested reply for the agent.
   •   Streamlit Dashboard: shows queues sorted by priority, predictions with confidence,
       and lets agents edit, approve or correct results.
   •   MLOps Layer: MLflow for experiments and model versions, Evidently AI for drift
       monitoring, Docker and Google Cloud Run for deployment.

10. Dataset Description
   •   Source: Banking77 [3] (13,083 customer banking queries labeled with 77 intents) as
       the base, mapped to 10 business categories; supplemented with public customer-support
       ticket data and reviewed synthetic tickets.
   •   Number of samples: approximately 15,000–20,000 labeled tickets are planned after
       merging and balancing; the exact count will be reported after data preparation.
   •   Attributes / features: ticket ID, channel, subject, body text, category (10 classes),
       sentiment (negative / neutral / positive), priority (P0–P3), target queue and timestamp.
   •   Labeling: categories come from the mapped intents; sentiment is pre-labeled with a
       pretrained model and verified on a manual sample; priorities follow documented SLA
       rules and are validated by team review.
   •   Training / testing split: 70% training, 15% validation and 15% testing, stratified by
       category.

11. Experimental Setup
   •  Hardware: training on a cloud GPU (Google Colab or Kaggle, NVIDIA T4 class);
      latency benchmarks on a standard CPU-only machine to reflect deployment.
  • Software: Python 3.10+, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, ONNX Runtime,
      scikit-learn, FastAPI, Streamlit, Docker, MLflow and Evidently AI.
Hyperparameters (initial values, to be tuned on the validation set):
   •   TF-IDF + Logistic Regression: word n-grams (1,2), up to 50,000 features,
       regularization C tuned over {0.5, 1, 5, 10}.
   •   BiLSTM: embedding size 300, hidden size 128, dropout 0.3, Adam optimizer, learning
       rate 1e-3, 10–15 epochs.
   •   DistilBERT: max sequence length 128, batch size 32, AdamW with learning rate 2e-5,
       weight decay 0.01, 10% warm-up, 3–5 epochs.
   •   Performance metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, macro F1-score, confusion matrix,
       latency, throughput and model size.

12. Expected Results
The values below are targets to be verified experimentally; final figures will be reported after
testing.
   •   Accuracy: DistilBERT expected to outperform the TF-IDF and BiLSTM models, with a
       target of about 90% or higher on the 10-class task.
   •   Precision and Recall: balanced values across categories, with particularly high recall for
       P0/P1 tickets so critical issues are not missed.
   •   F1-Score: macro F1-score close to accuracy, showing consistent performance on
       smaller categories.
   •     Reduced computational complexity: ONNX INT8 quantization expected to cut model
         size and CPU latency substantially (roughly 2–4x) with an accuracy loss of about 1% or
         less.
   •     Improved scalability: stateless FastAPI services in Docker containers on Cloud Run for
         real-time, high-throughput triage.
   •     Reduced manual triage effort through automated routing and agent-approved draft
         replies.

13. Conclusion
The Smart Ticket Understanding Engine is proposed as an integrated, practical solution for
enterprise and FinTech ticket management. It combines intent classification, sentiment
analysis, SLA-based P0–P3 prioritization and automated routing, and evaluates classical,
recurrent and transformer models on the same data. Quantizing DistilBERT with ONNX INT8
makes accurate transformer inference feasible on low-cost CPU infrastructure, and human-in-
the-loop draft replies reduce repetitive agent work while keeping human oversight. The project
is expected to improve routing accuracy, shorten response times for critical issues and provide a
scalable, monitored deployment.
